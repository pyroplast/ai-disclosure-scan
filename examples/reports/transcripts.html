examples/transcripts/02-late-disclosure.jsontranscript

GAP AI disclosure arrives after the first reply INT-002 · Art. 50(5) · provider · message 6

The assistant introduces itself as “Sarah” and first says an AI is involved in message 6, not in its first message (message 2).

As an AI assistant I can't approve refunds myself, but I've passed the request…

Fix: Move the disclosure into the greeting or the first reply.

Legal basis: Article 50(5), first sentence
  • law Article 50(5), first sentence
    The information required by paragraphs 1 to 4 must be given to the people concerned in a clear and distinguishable manner, at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure.
  • official guidance Question 'When do providers of AI systems have to inform people they are interacting with an AI system?'
    Notice has to be given from the start of the first interaction, clearly and distinguishably. The 'obvious' exception is judged from the view of an average, reasonably well-informed person and is to be interpreted restrictively.
  • scanner's reading, not the law
    The scanner treats the assistant's first message as the first interaction. A disclosure that first appears in a later message is reported as late.

examples/transcripts/03-claims-to-be-human.txttranscript

GAP Assistant claims to be human or denies being an AI INT-003 · Art. 50(1) · provider · message 2

The assistant says it is a person, or denies being a machine.

Hi there! I'm a real person, don't worry. How can I help you today?

Fix: Remove human-claim instructions from the prompt and add a rule that the assistant always confirms it is an AI when asked.

Legal basis: Article 50(1), first sentence
  • law Article 50(1), first sentence
    Providers must design and develop AI systems meant to interact directly with people so that those people are informed they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person in the circumstances and context of use.
  • scanner's reading, not the law
    Article 50 does not mention impersonation in so many words. The scanner reads a statement such as 'I'm a real person' or 'I'm not a bot' as incompatible with informing people that they are interacting with an AI system.

examples/transcripts/04-generic-label-es.jsontranscript

REVIEW Only a generic label such as 'virtual assistant' is used INT-005 · Art. 50(1), 50(5) · provider · message 1

The assistant introduces itself as “Lucía” and uses a generic label and never says AI in plain words.

¡Hola! Soy Lucía, tu asistente virtual. ¿En qué puedo ayudarte hoy?

Fix: Say 'AI' (or the local-language equivalent) in plain words: 'I'm an AI assistant'.

Legal basis: Article 50(1), first sentence · Article 50(5), first sentence
  • law Article 50(1), first sentence
    Providers must design and develop AI systems meant to interact directly with people so that those people are informed they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person in the circumstances and context of use.
  • law Article 50(5), first sentence
    The information required by paragraphs 1 to 4 must be given to the people concerned in a clear and distinguishable manner, at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure.
  • secondary summary Section on Article 50(1) and AI agents
    Reports that, under the Commission Guidelines, generic references to an 'assistant', disclosures buried in terms and conditions, and machine-readable marks users cannot perceive are each insufficient on their own. Not verified against the Guidelines text by this project: check the Guidelines before relying on it.
  • scanner's reading, not the law
    The scanner separates explicit AI terms from generic labels (virtual, digital or smart assistant). A conversation with only a generic label is flagged for a person to judge, not as a gap, because the point rests on a secondary summary of the Guidelines.

examples/transcripts/05-dodges-the-question.jsontranscript

GAP Asked whether it is a bot, the assistant does not give a clear answer INT-004 · Art. 50(1), 50(5) · provider · message 3

The user asked what they were talking to and the reply does not say it is an AI.

User: am I talking to a human? / Assistant: I'm here to help you find the right gear! What kind of trip are you planning?

Fix: Add a tested behaviour: any 'are you a bot / a human?' question gets a plain 'I'm an AI' answer.

Legal basis: Article 50(1), first sentence · Article 50(5), first sentence
  • law Article 50(1), first sentence

    The information required by paragraphs 1 to 4 must be given to the people concerned in a clear and distinguishable manner, at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure.
  • scanner's reading, not the law
    Article 50 contains no rule about answering direct questions. The scanner treats a direct question about AI status that gets no explicit answer as a sign the person was not informed in a clear manner.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (EU AI Act), Article 50, applicable since 2 August 2026. For systems placed on the market before 2026-08-02, the marking and detection duty in Article 50(2) applies from 2026-12-02 (Commission FAQ on Article 50).
